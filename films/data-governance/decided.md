# Decided: Data governance, off the shelf

The series' own decisions. With each film's rows (`<n-film>/decided.md`), they make the *Decided* table in [proposal.md](proposal.md): run `python site-tools/build_series.py` after adding a row. A decision a film's thread makes, even one for the whole series, goes in that film's file, so threads never edit the same lines.

| Date | Decision |
|---|---|
| 16 October 2026 | A series on data governance, and a companion series on master and reference data. Both align with the series on definitions and modelling (*From words to data*, *In the weeds of data crafting*). |
| 16 October 2026 | DAMA-DMBOK is the reference body. It's named once, in the opening film; after that, the series teaches what to do, not the framework's vocabulary. |
| 16 October 2026 | Very practical, with no over-conceptualisation. The series shows governance **enablers**: things in the work that make the right thing the easy thing, and enforce it. Not assessments, plans and heavy documentation with low enforcement. |
| 16 October 2026 | More films on data quality (another film, on fixing at the source) and on data product ownership. |
| 16 October 2026 | A second part, *With the tools*: films on Databricks and dbt that implement the governance elements, each expanding the films of the first part it names. It includes data products and a marketplace with open formats for contracts and products, with Ontos (Databricks Labs) as the example. |
| 16 October 2026 | Governance is about people and processes first. Tools help, and the series shows them, but governance is human work: understanding, motivation, change management, support and guidance. Every film shows its people side, and the last film, *Teachers of data*, is about it. |
| 16 October 2026 | The governance team are the teachers of data, where all the pieces come together, as DAMA's wheel shows with governance at its centre. The proposal maps which of DAMA's knowledge areas each series and film covers. |
| 27 October 2026 | The key challenge in data governance, and its priority, is people's engagement, not only their agreement: communication, so people understand data and its governance (data literacy); taking part; owning responsibility and accountability; being willing to decide, and to respect the other authorities; and doing the duties of a role. All of it is harder without practical tools that give people guidance, visibility and feedback on the impact of their work. Governance has been hard partly because the technology was split across many places, so the work was disorganised and hard to see; the tools films show that changing. People stay at the core, and matter more as the tools improve, just as governance stays at the core of data management and matters more as data management gets easier to run. *Teachers of data*, the film on the people side, sets this out, as the author asked after watching *One word, one home*. |
| 28 October 2026 | Every opening story has four beats, each said outright in the narration: the scenario; the problem (what went wrong or what people feared, for whom, and why it mattered to them, with every word the story leans on explained); the solution; and the benefits (what got better, then the same for data governance). The film comes back to the story along the way and closes on its benefit. The author asked for one rule every film follows after *Who may see it*'s script set the scene of the 1983 census without saying why it was a problem. The rule is in AGENTS.md and PLAYBOOK §2 for every series; each script quotes its beats in an *Opening story* table, *Off the shelf*'s first, and `check_site.py` fails a film whose table misses a beat. |
| 28 October 2026 | Three series in place of one, as the author asked, one for each pack of 21 October: *Governance principles* (*Principios de gobernanza*), the principles of data governance, with the prologue and films 1 to 10 of the plan; *Governance on the map* (*La gobernanza en el mapa*), governance designed into the university's architecture map, with films 11 to 13; and *Governance you can use* (*Gobernanza que puedes usar*), governance made operational and useful beyond the policies, with films 14 to 19. Each series has its own folder, page and *Decided* table, and follows from the one before, under the topic *Data governance*. The plan in [proposal.md](proposal.md) stays the one for all three, and the films keep their numbers in it; each series numbers its own films on its cards: "Film 1 of 10" to "Film 10 of 10" here, "Film 1 of 3" on the map, and "Film 1 of 6" in the third. The first series keeps this folder and its page, so no film's address changes, and the example dbt project stays in `analytics-engineering/project/`. |
| 3 November 2026 | The series' labs and scenarios cover the prologue and all ten films, as the author asked for each governance series ("similar to master data"): 25 labs (*Take it apart*) and 54 situations (*Make the call*), in English and Spanish. Each lab follows one of its film's chapters, and each situation sits on a lab of its own film, numbered by film and place (the prologue counts as film 0, so its four are 1 to 4). Like the films, they show no technical files or tool details: no code, YAML, SQL, pull requests, file paths or tool screens; only papers people read, such as a change request, a sign-off sheet, a register, a scorecard or a check. They use the master data labs' format and kinds (sort, pick, steps, compose), with their own words and engine in `site/assets/data-governance/`, drawn with the series' watercolour look and people. The other two governance series keep their own folders, so the three can change apart. |
| 3 November 2026 | The labs are on the site: the series' page has the course bar and a section that leads to them, the site's page builder makes their pages in English and Spanish, and each film's page and *Where next?* panel offers its own. With all ten films on the site, films 1 to 9 no longer say more are on the way. The site's browser checks play every lab and every situation on a phone. |
| 4 November 2026 | Fewer labs and situations, as the author asked: under ten each per series, keeping the most interactive and relevant so people stay engaged. 9 labs and 9 situations, down from 25 and 54. Kept, one lab and one situation for each of films 1 to 10 but film 3: *Governance or management?*, *Owner, steward, custodian*, *A purpose and a date*, *Whose level?*, *Follow it back*, *A change that surprises no one*, *Write the promise*, *What the agent may do*, *The mime before the fine*. Hands-on kinds (compose, steps) came first, then the idea each film turns on. The prologue and film 3 keep their Pause and think questions; the glossary is practised in the other two series' labs. |
