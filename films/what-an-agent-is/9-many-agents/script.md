# What an agent is · Many agents, and what emerges: script

*The script of* Many agents, and what emerges*, film 9 of 9 of* What an agent is*, the first series of the module on AI agents in data work, in seven chapters, in English, in the module's marble-and-oak look. Written on 3 November 2026 from the series' briefs (section 1.9), after Rodrigo asked for the nine films to be made without a script review (3 November 2026, 08:11). It follows the four-beat rule for every opening (AGENTS.md, *Story and world*). Every claim is checked against its source on the day (*Rigour*, *Sources*). The university and its people are the series' invented ones; the opening's building, its architect and the research are real.*

## The promise

Every line passes two tests: someone who has never used an AI agent can follow it, and a complexity scientist, an engineer who builds systems of many agents and a biologist each find nothing wrong in it. Film 1.8 looked at one agent and one mind. This film looks at many: swarms of agents are coming fast, splitting work, checking each other and producing results no single agent planned. Nature has done this for a long time, in termite mounds, ant trails and starling flocks; it happens in our own neurons and in markets, where it can help, or go wrong in ways nobody can trace. Crowds of agents look alive, but by the best answer we have, a living thing makes itself, and today's agents don't. Nobody knows what many agents will do together. So the film ends where the opening story ends: something that works in ways its builders didn't understand, kept working because people measured it and a person decided. **When many work together, something new appears, and we don't yet know what.**

## The story in one paragraph

Harare, Zimbabwe, 1992: a property company asks the architect Mick Pearce for offices and shops in the city centre. A building that size would be cooled by imported, expensive air conditioning, running all day: high bills, and high rents for the shops and offices inside. The owners ask for a building that cools itself. Pearce looks to termite mounds, then thought to breathe like chimneys, and builds Eastgate the same way, with many simple parts instead of one big machine: on cool nights fans pull air in and concrete under the floors stores the cool; by day warm air rises out through 48 brick chimneys. It opens in 1996 with no air conditioning; it costs about a tenth less to build and uses far less energy, rents can be lower, and its shops and offices are let before it's finished. At the university, after each census day, the council asks why each faculty's count changed; the reasons sit in four faculties' notes and emails, and last year gathering them took two weeks, after the budgets were set. This year many small agents share the work. The film then shows how many simple parts make something none of them planned (termites, ants, starlings, John Holland's "more comes out than was put in"), where nobody can trace why (thought from neurons; the market crash of 6 May 2010), today's teams of agents (a lead agent, helpers and a checking agent; better on hard research, at many times the computing, and failing often; a group that settles on one name and leans in a way no single agent does), and the university's afternoon: read-only helpers in a sandbox pin what they find to one shared board; on it, three faculties' small drops turn out to be the same 31 students, moved to a new joint degree that the count put in no faculty, and a wrong word, "dropouts", spreads from one helper to every draft until the checking agent flags it. Clara, in Planning, fixes the word and approves the reasons; the degree gets its faculty, and a new test catches the next one without an agent. Is a crowd of agents alive? Maturana and Varela said a living thing makes itself; Grey Walter's robot tortoise kept itself going; frog cells shaped to a computer's design swam, healed and gathered new ones; today's agents do none of this. An imagined future, labelled as imagined. Then back to Harare: sensors in real mounds later showed the air moves less like a chimney than a lung, so Eastgate was built on the wrong idea, and still it works, because people measured it and tuned it. Jun's team keeps its agents the same way. The film hands over to the next series, *Agents at work on data*.

## The look

*Marble and oak*, the module's look (chosen 3 November 2026): a pale grey marble wall with soft diagonal veins, a slim white skirting, a pale oak floor, daylight from a window on the right, matte off-white cards with soft shadows. No film grain. Average lightness about 73 of 100.

- **The series' shared pieces** (film 1.1's setup, `films/what-an-agent-is/shared/src/`, prefix `ag_`): the room, Jun, the agent sphere at any size, the cards. Anything this film needs beyond them (the shared board, the oak tray, the lock, the outside scenes) is drawn in its own `src/plan.js`, never in the shared code.
- **Colours carry the series' meaning:** blue for tools and structure (the platform's count, the lock, the test), violet for the model thinking (the agents' bands), orange for data coming back (the source chips), green for a check that passes, amber for a check that fails. Outside scenes don't use these colours for anything else: warm air is a pale terracotta, cool air a pale mist grey, the ants' scent a faint pale gold.
- **Scenes outside data fill the frame,** matte and softly lit, with no outlines, in muted Nordic colours, daylight from the right and soft contact shadows, each with its place and year in the top-left corner: "Harare, Zimbabwe · 1992", "Termite mounds · southern Africa", "A laboratory · 1989", "Rome · winter 2005 to 2006", "United States · 6 May 2010", "Santiago, Chile · 1972", "Near Bristol · about 1950", "United States · 2020 and 2021", "India and Namibia · 2015 and 2017". - **No real person's face.** Mick Pearce is his hands at a drawing board and a name card; Maturana and Varela are their names under the cell; Grey Walter is his tortoise. People in the 1992 street are small, at a distance, dressed for their time. At the university, Jun (the series' Jun) and Clara Jensen, in Planning, drawn to the series' rules for people (an adult's proportions: a full-size head, eyes halfway down it, short hair that follows the skull, a neck about half as wide as the head is tall, shoulders a third of a head below the chin).
- **Many agents read as many.** A crowd of spheres is drawn from one sphere at many sizes, each with its own slight delay and settle, so no two move in step until the film means them to.
- **Words on screen** are the narration's names and numbers, short. The board's cards hold a faculty, a number and a reason, never a student's name.

### 2 · Much from little · 1:52–2:41

**Narration.** No termite holds the plan of a mound. Each drops its mud where its nestmates' scent is strongest, so pillars rise, then arches. The work itself tells the next worker what to do. Ants leave scent too. Given two bridges to food, most soon take the shorter, where scent builds up faster, and their trails become the colony's shared memory. Starlings over Rome turn together in flocks of thousands, each bird watching only six or seven neighbours. No bird leads. This is emergence: the whole does what no part planned. As John Holland, who studied it for decades, put it: more comes out than was put in.

### 4 · Many agents · 3:24–4:23

**Narration.** Now we are building crowds of a new kind, many AI agents working together, and they are coming fast. Often, a lead agent splits the job among helpers, each with its own instructions, and another agent checks what they find. Such teams can beat one agent on hard research, at many times the computing. Yet a 2025 study of seven such systems found them failing on forty-one to eighty-seven percent of tasks. And together, they do things no one planned. In 2025, researchers paired agents at random, again and again, to agree on a name. With no one in charge, the whole group settled on one. And the group leaned towards some names, though no agent did alone. So testing each agent alone isn't enough: test the group.

### 6 · Is it alive? · 5:48–6:36

**Narration.** Crowds of agents can seem alive. Are they? In Chile, in 1972, Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela said a living thing makes itself: a cell keeps making the parts that make it. ## Sound

A new palette for the series' last film, checked against every score on main on 3 November 2026 (the series' films list theirs in `series-1/palettes.md`, so none repeats): **many small voices** and **one low one**. The small voices are soft, pure, rounded notes, each a short tone that blooms and fades like a drop, made in the score; each sounds where something small appears on screen (a part of Eastgate, a pellet of mud, an ant, a bird, an agent). At first each comes in its own time, scattered across left and right; then they fall into step and become one chord: the film's mark for emergence. The low voice is a bass clarinet, low and soft, one note at a time, held steady, with no vibrato (a bowed double bass would repeat *Change without surprises*; the bass clarinet's one other use, in *One row of what and when*, is in another series, with other voices). E flat major, with the Lydian A natural at the turns, for wonder; every chord tuned pure.

- **Under the opening,** the bass clarinet holds the home note over Harare. The air-conditioning plant is a low hum (an effect, under 200 Hz) that runs while the dial turns and stops on "Something had to change". At the mound, the small voices start one by one, scattered; as Eastgate's parts light, each part is a voice, and on "By day" they fall into step: the chord. On "1996", the chord resolves. At the university, the voices scatter again as the agents roll in, and fall into step on "drafts by the afternoon"; the amber flag is two low notes falling a fourth. The title card: the series' mark, as the series thread sets it.
- **After the opening,** music comes only at a chapter's turn, under a moment on screen, or in a pause without words; never a bed under the voice. The pellets, the ants and the birds are small voices, each where it appears, joining into the chord on "This is emergence". The neurons are the softest voices, with no chord. The market crash is the only place with a pulse: low and muted, about 96 beats a minute, from "did something no one planned" to "then came back", where it stops (PLAYBOOK, *Let rhythm follow the feeling*: an error). The naming study is pairs of voices, two at a time, until the whole crowd sounds one note; the group's lean is the bass clarinet alone. On the board, each pinned card is a soft tap; the checking agent's tick is a rising fifth, its flag the two falling notes; Clara's stamp is the chord. The cell is a slow ring of voices; the tortoise a low motor and a soft click as it docks; the frog cells water-soft voices; the switch, a click and silence. Back in Harare, the bass clarinet returns; on "and still, it works", the chord; under the takeaway, the voices fall into step one last time and end on the Lydian chord, left open.

## Rigour to check before recording

Checked on 3 November 2026. The research notes, with the quotes each line rests on, are in `notes.md` beside this script.

- **The opening, Harare.** Commission 1992, architect Mick Pearce, "a two-building office complex and shopping mall" in Harare (ASME, 2019). The client "asked Pearce for a building that didn't use expensive air conditioning, that would cool itself by passive means" (IPS, 4 June 1996); the film calls the client "a property company", unnamed (AGENTS.md: no real company as the subject). "Imported, expensive" air conditioning: "high-energy, imported, expensive air conditioning systems" (IPS). High rents: IPS, "Because Eastgate was cheaper to build and to service, it can charge lower rentals". Pearce grew up in Zimbabwe, knew the mounds' chimneys, and had seen a termite nest's chimney on television (ASME); the chimney idea, warm air rising and drawing air in from below, is Pearce's own account (ASME: "You get air rising out the chimney, and that pulls air in from the holes"); "thought then" because later research found otherwise (below). Fans pull night air in; concrete blocks with teeth in ducts under each floor store the cool; warm air leaves through the atrium and 48 brick chimneys along the roofs (ASME; IPS: "48 thick brick chimneys", "cool night air is pulled in"). "Many simple parts instead of one big machine": fans, concrete and chimneys in place of an air-conditioning plant; the fans are machines, small ones. Opened 1996 (Wikipedia, completion 1996; IPS describes it complete in June 1996), with no air conditioning (IPS compares it with air-conditioned buildings; ASME: "than a similarly sized building with air conditioning"). "About a tenth less to build": ASME, "10 percent less to build than a similarly sized building with air conditioning". "Far less energy": the sources agree it uses far less and disagree on how much (IPS: "five percent of the energy a conventional air conditioning system would"; Wikipedia, citing Pearce: 10%; ASME's sentence is garbled, "used 35 percent energy when comparable buildings"), so the film gives no number for energy. "Let before it was finished": IPS, "Shops and offices were rented before completion". Harare's cool nights: ASME, 4,800 ft, a swing of about 10 °C a day.
- **The data story.** The university, Jun, Clara Jensen in Planning, and the census date, 31 March 2026, are the series' and the example project's (`dbt_project.yml`, `census_date`). The four faculties are the example project's: Arts and Education, Business, Engineering and IT, Health. The counts on screen add up to film 1.2's census-day count, 18,412 students (17,958 the year before): Arts and Education 4,236, Business 6,018, Engineering and IT 5,247 and Health 2,880 (film 1.3's number for the Faculty of Health this term), 18,381 in all, and 31 in no faculty. Last year's: 4,102, 5,861, 5,066 and 2,929. The 31 came from Business (9), Engineering and IT (13) and Health (9). A degree with no faculty falls out of every faculty's row and stays in the university's total, so the faculties add up to 31 fewer than the total; the example project's course model has no test that every award has a faculty (`models/core/course/_core_course__models.yml`, `faculty_code`), which is why the new test is the right fix. "Studying, inactive, withdrawn or completed" is the learner's status in the example project's canonical set (`models/core/student/_student__columns.md`, line 36). The rules the story keeps: counts made deterministically, agents only for the words (the module's rule: deterministic automation first); helpers read-only, in a sandbox; one shared board as the team's memory; the checking agent flags, Clara approves (agents are never approvers). The two weeks, the afternoon and the budgets are the story's own.
- **Much from little.** Termites drop pellets of mud laced with scent where their nestmates' scent is strongest; positive feedback builds pillars, then arches (the classic model, as Wikipedia's *Stigmergy* gives it, citing Beckers, O. Holland and Deneubourg, 1994). Grassé named it stigmergy in 1959: "stimulation of workers by the performance they have achieved" (*Insectes Sociaux* 6: 41-80); the film says it in plain words and doesn't use the term. Ants and the two bridges: Goss, Aron, Deneubourg and Pasteels (1989), Argentine ants; when one bridge is much shorter, most ants come to use it, because the first to return took it and its pheromone builds up sooner (Dorigo, *Scholarpedia*). Trails as "a shared external memory for the ant colony" (Wikipedia, *Stigmergy*, citing Blum and Merkle, 2008). Starlings: Ballerini and others (*PNAS*, 2008), flocks of "a few thousand" over Rome, "each bird interacts on average with a fixed number of neighbors (six to seven)"; "no bird leads" is the field's reading of such flocks, which have no leader. "The whole does what no part planned" is the film's definition, in plain words. Holland: "more comes out than was put in" (*Emergence*, 1998, p. 225); he studied complex adaptive systems from the 1960s until 2015.
- **Where nobody can trace why.** About 86 billion neurons in an adult human brain (Azevedo and others, 2009: 86.1 ± 8.1 billion). No account yet traces a thought from the activity of neurons; film 1.8 asks what that means. The market crash of 6 May 2010: the joint report of the SEC and CFTC (30 September 2010): prices "plummeted a further 5-6% in a matter of minutes before rebounding almost as quickly"; a large automated sell order "without regard to price or time"; high-frequency traders "quickly buy and then resell contracts to each other", a "hot-potato" volume effect; trades at a penny. "Five months": 6 May to 30 September. "People still argue": the report's account was disputed, a CFTC study (Kirilenko and others; its 2014 version, published in 2017) found high-frequency traders contributed but didn't cause it, and in 2015 a trader was charged and the CFTC said he was "at least significantly responsible for the order imbalances"; none is named. "In America": the futures traded in Chicago and the shares in New York.
- **Many agents.** "Coming fast": systems of many agents are among the main technology trends named for 2026 by analysts (October 2025); not named in the film. A lead agent that splits the work for helpers working in parallel, and an agent that checks: a common pattern (Anthropic's research system, 13 June 2025: a lead agent "decomposes" a query and gives subagents tasks; it beat a single agent by 90.2% on an internal research evaluation, and such systems use "about 15× more tokens than chats"); the film names no product. "Failing on forty-one to eighty-seven percent of tasks": Cemri and others (2025), seven open-source systems, 1,600+ traces, "41% to 86.7%". The naming study: Ashery, Aiello and Baronchelli (*Science Advances*, 14 May 2025): groups of 24 agents, two chosen at random each round to pick a name from a pool, rewarded when they match; a shared convention emerged with no one in charge, and "strong collective biases can emerge ... even when agents exhibit no bias individually"; "the safety of a single LLM does not necessarily imply the safety of a multi-agent system" (the film: "testing each agent alone isn't enough").
- **Is it alive?** Autopoiesis: Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela, Chile, 1972 (*De máquinas y seres vivos*); a living system is "a network of processes of production ... of components which ... continuously regenerate and realize the network of processes (relations) that produced them" (1980, p. 78); the film's "a cell keeps making the parts that make it". Grey Walter's tortoises, *Machina speculatrix*, built at the Burden Neurological Institute near Bristol in 1948 and 1949; in the 1950 newsreel, "when her batteries begin to fail, she automatically runs home to her kennel for charging up"; "around 1950" covers both. The frog-cell bodies (xenobots): skin and heart cells of *Xenopus laevis*, designed by an evolutionary algorithm on a supercomputer and built by hand (University of Vermont and Tufts, 13 January 2020), under a millimetre, "heal themselves after being cut", and live about a week; in 2021 (Wyss Institute, 29 November 2021) some gathered loose cells into new ones that did the same. "Today's agents do none of this": they are made, started and stopped by people, and don't make their own parts.
- **What we don't know.** The imagined future is said to be imagined and tagged "Imagined". The later research: King, Ocko and Mahadevan (*PNAS*, 2015), mounds in India, use "diurnal ambient temperature oscillations for ventilation"; Ocko and others (*Journal of Experimental Biology*, 2017), Macrotermes michaelseni in Namibia, "harness periodic solar heating to drive ventilation ... functioning as an external lung". "Built on the wrong idea, a scientist said": Guy Theraulaz, "Eastgate Centre was built on a wrong conception of how termites regulate the temperature of their nests ... Still, Eastgate works." (ASME). Tuned: offices reached 80 to 82 °F late in the day for several weeks a year, which "led Pearce and Ove Arup to improve the control system" (ASME). The takeaway is the brief's, word for word. The next series is *Agents at work on data* (the plan).
- **Not shown.** Swarm robotics; the Kuramoto model and fireflies; flocks in other species; open-ended evolution and artificial life in software; the termite mound research's details (tomography, CO2); any product or framework for systems of many agents.
